Imaginary IQ-focused practice conversation: both models were asked to explain 3x + 5 = 20 in Tagalog. The observations and timestamps below are invented solely to demonstrate formatting, not evidence from a real task. Do not submit this example as your own evaluation.

Overall Preference: Model A Preferred

Decided Mainly by: IQ-focused, Utility

Reasoning: MA explained each step and checked the answer in T2. MB gave the correct answer but skipped the checking step. MA was more helpful for a beginner who wanted to understand the process.

Connect the overall choice to the scenario priority and a meaningful difference.

NEA: Model B Preferred

Evidence: In this fictional recording, MB used a warmer, more conversational tone in T2, while MA sounded more formal and flat.

NEA needs real listening evidence in an actual evaluation. A transcript cannot establish vocal tone.

Utility: Model A Preferred

Evidence: MA showed how to subtract 5, divide by 3, and substitute x = 5 back into the equation in T2. MB also found x = 5 but did not check the result. MA gave a more complete explanation.

Name the turn and explain why the difference affects the user's goal.

CD: Tie, Both Good

Evidence: In this fictional recording, both models waited until the user finished asking the question and responded to the follow-up without interruptions or unusual pauses.

Judge turn-taking from actual recordings, not from the appearance of a transcript.

AQ: Model A Preferred

Evidence: MA had fewer technical audio issues than MB in this fictional recording.

Model A: No audible artifacts observed.
Model B: A brief tick at T2 0:09 and warbling at T4 0:12.

Use model, turn, timestamp and artifact type. These timestamps are fictional examples.

Task Success: Both Models Passed

Evidence: Both models solved the equation correctly and explained the basic steps. MA added a useful verification step, but MB still met the main objective in this fictional scenario.

Pass/fail is separate from preference. A weaker answer can still meet the main goal.

Model A: No error cluster found in this fictional example.
Model B: No error cluster established from the stated observations.

NEA — Naturalness, engagement and aesthetics

Listen for natural speech, emotional fit, pronunciation and expressive delivery. Text can show wording, but it cannot prove vocal tone.

Utility — Relevance and accuracy

Check whether the response actually helps, follows instructions, covers the goal and avoids unsupported factual claims.

CD — Conversational dynamics

Listen for how turns are exchanged, whether a model responds at the right time and whether interruptions or long pauses affect the interaction.

AQ — Audio quality

Listen for technical sound defects such as warbling, ticks, pops, distortion or glitches. Do not confuse intentional vocal expression with a technical artifact.

Task success — Pass or fail

Judge the main objective according to the official criteria. A minor error is not automatically a failure. If evidence is missing, do not claim it was observed.

Error clusters — Patterns of errors

Use the official category definitions and supporting examples. Do not assume a category merely because a single phrase sounds repetitive.

Fair comparison — Keep conditions similar

Use comparable topics, follow-ups, energy and length. Respect the scenario requirements, including any specified number of turns.

Factual verification — Check claims

Use reliable sources where the project allows or requires verification. Distinguish a missing source from a confirmed factual error.
